# Design philosophy

Principles for judging a change that the rules delta does not spell out. The delta remains the authority for what actually changed. This file records why, so a later edit can follow the same goal.

## Wider level bands

Pathfinder adds your full level to every trained check. A few levels of difference move the math so far that earlier creatures become trivial and creatures a few levels up become unfair. Adventures have to stay inside a narrow band.

Wyrmway keeps proficiency ranks, and shrinks the part that grows with level. The numbers are in [rules-delta.md](rules-delta.md). The aim is a curve close to D&D 5e's proficiency bonus at trained rank, with expert, master, and legendary as smaller steps across the range where 5e has a single expertise double. Some scaling remains. A stretch of an adventure, such as a troll war that lasts more than a few levels, stays playable without rebuilding every encounter.

Because both sides of a check use the same formula, an equal-level match keeps about the same chance it has in Pathfinder. The creature's armor class and the character's attack shrink together. What changes is the gap between levels: inside a four-level stretch the level term is flat, and across that stretch it rises by one.

Hit points, damage, ability boosts, and item bonuses still grow on Pathfinder's curve until a later decision says otherwise. Flattening proficiency does not by itself stop a higher-level striker from killing an older creature faster.

## Effects can settle a scene

Pathfinder writes spells, feats, items, and class features to protect a numerical balance. Each option is given a narrow job, then enough saves, traits, and exceptions that a clever use cannot decide what happens next. The encounter stays standing, and it ends when hit points reach zero. Players describe the result as casters brought down until nobody can do something memorable, with the martial left to finish the fight. Wyrmway treats a prepared, specific effect that ends, prevents, or reroutes a scene as a success of play.

The reference is Jester's cupcake, Critical Role campaign 2, episode 93. She persuaded a hag to share a cupcake dusted with an item that imposed disadvantage on Wisdom saves, then cast Modify Memory so the hag believed she had agreed to lift a curse. The save failed. The negotiation was over. Pathfinder's version of that spell, Rewrite Memory, does not allow the result. On a failed save the caster may edit six seconds of memory per round while sustaining, and the permanent version requires a willing target and only suppresses a topic. The text spends its length on what the spell will not settle.

Suggestions on a spell, feat, item, or class feature follow these tests.

- The effect may be strong enough to end, prevent, or reroute an encounter when the player has set it up. Setup is preparation, a combination with another feature, or a true reading of the situation. The cost of that power is the setup and the risk of the roll.
- The text says what the effect does. It does not list the clever uses it forbids. A limit stays when it is the shape of the action: what must be touched, who must hear it, how long it takes. A limit goes when its only job is to stop the scene from being solved.
- Spells are the first place to apply this. Pathfinder narrowed them to keep casters level with martials, and the fights that remain are won by taking hit points off. Items, feats, and class features get the same license, including martial and skill ones. The aim is that a clever character can settle a scene. It is not that only a caster's turn matters, and it is not that a caster's ordinary damage should exceed a martial's.
- An effect that simply says the target loses, with no setup and no specific fiction, is not the goal. The cupcake worked because the player brought an item, won the conversation, and the spell then did the strong thing it claimed.

The incapacitation trait is removed. It made the important creature permanently harder to affect, so a disabling effect was fishing for a natural 1. The replacement is a spendable shield on the creature, not a restriction written into the spell, feat, or item. An elite creature has one legendary save. A boss has a handful of legendary saves and legendary actions, and the GM can add more for a larger party. The numbers and the timing are in [rules-delta.md](rules-delta.md).

A legendary save is temporary. The party forces failures until the creature spends its uses, and then the effect resolves at the degree that was rolled. That is the timer on the fight. A legendary action is the other dial: the boss acts between turns, so a single creature can be a fight without a crowd of minions whose only job is to fill the action economy.

## Accuracy is not the class dial

Pathfinder makes a class weaker by delaying its proficiency ranks, or by stopping it short of master or legendary. The fighter starts a rank ahead with weapons and stays there. The gunslinger gets the same head start with firearms. A full caster waits until 7th for expert spell attacks, 15th for master, and 19th for legendary, and never receives the item bonus a weapon does. Players aim for a comfortable success rate on the action that defines the character, roughly two times in three against a fair challenge, higher when the task is easy or the character is specialized, lower against a boss. They then pick whichever option actually hits that often. A fireball that deals half on a successful save registers as a miss next to a strike that hits and crits. The class that was given the better accuracy becomes the class people play.

Wyrmway does not use accuracy to balance a class. A character's core weapon proficiency, or their core spell attack and spell DC, advances on one shared schedule: trained at 1st, expert at 5th, master at 9th, and legendary at 15th. A fighter and a wizard doing their jobs are on the same rank. Spell attacks and spell DCs receive an item bonus, because "target the weakest save" only works when the caster has a spell for that save, knows which save it is, and the best defense is not Armor Class.

A narrow specialty may sit ahead of that schedule, to carry flavor and a real choice. One weapon group for a fighter or gunslinger, chosen at 1st, or one school for a wizard. The lead stays inside a single rank, so one character is never trained or expert while another is master or legendary at the same job. Whether that lead exists, and how long the extra +2 lasts, is an experiment.

## Crafting is worth doing

Pathfinder wrote Craft to stop organized-play tables from turning downtime into free items. The cost is that a character who invests in Crafting still finishes an item more slowly and more expensively than someone who Earns Income and buys it. Feats mostly answer a GM who refuses to sell a common item. A class built on consumables then needs a separate daily batch of free items, because paying the listed price every day does not work.

Wyrmway treats crafting as a way to make the item, and a reason to do it instead of buying it. Consumables come down in price so that making them is a normal part of play. A crafting class can be about what it makes and how well, rather than about a free stack that exists only because the prices are too high. The procedure and the new prices are a later rules pass.

## Common feats are printed once

Pathfinder reprints the same feat for each class or ancestry that should have it. Reactive Strike lists a trait for every class that offers it. Sudden Charge does the same. An archetype that wants a basic class option often prints its own copy, because a multiclass dedication only grants that class's feats up to half the character's level. Starfinder already stopped doing this for ancestries: one standardized feat, such as Ancestral Weapon Familiarity, and the ancestries that should have it simply can take it.

Wyrmway does the same for ancestry feats and for class feats. A repeated option becomes one feat in a shared pool. Access comes from a tag the class, archetype, or ancestry grants, not from a new copy of the text. A martial tag opens the warrior pool. A spellcaster tag opens the spellcaster pool, and a tradition tag can narrow a feat further. Ancestry options that every people might have, such as ancestral lore or ancestral weaponry, live in one basic pool.

A feat that exists only because of one class or one ancestry stays there. A feat that becomes available to everyone with a tag may need its power adjusted for that wider access. Which feats move, what the tags are named, and which ones change power, is a later pass over the actual lists.
